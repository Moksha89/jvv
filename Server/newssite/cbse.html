Chapter 1 • Chemistry
Chemical Reactions and Equations
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Chemical equation: Symbolic representation of a chemical reaction
  • Balanced equation: Equal number of atoms on both sides
  • Types of reactions: Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Double Displacement, Oxidation-Reduction
  • Exothermic reaction: Releases heat (e.g., burning of natural gas)
  • Endothermic reaction: Absorbs heat (e.g., decomposition of calcium carbonate)
  • Corrosion: Metal surface attacked by substances like moisture and acids
  • Rancidity: Oxidation of fats and oils causing bad smell

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (Combination)
2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃ (Decomposition)
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (Displacement)
Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl (Double Displacement)
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O (Redox)

  3. What happens when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to iron filings?
  4. Why do we apply paint on iron articles?

Chapter 2 • Chemistry
Acids, Bases and Salts
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Acid: Substance that produces H⁺ ions in water (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄)
  • Base: Substance that produces OH⁻ ions in water (e.g., NaOH, KOH)
  • pH Scale: Measures hydrogen ion concentration (0-14); pH 7 is neutral
  • Indicators: Litmus, phenolphthalein, methyl orange change color with acids/bases
  • Neutralization: Acid + Base → Salt + Water
  • Preparation of Bleaching Powder: Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O
  • Preparation of Baking Soda: NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ + NH₃ → NH₄Cl + NaHCO₃
  • Plaster of Paris: CaSO₄·½H₂O, used for fractured bones

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O (Neutralization)
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
2NaHCO₃ →(heat) Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂
CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O (Plaster of Paris setting)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  3. What is the importance of pH in everyday life?

Chapter 3 • Chemistry
Metals and Non-Metals
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Physical properties: Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors; Non-metals are brittle, poor conductors
  • Reactivity Series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Ni > Sn > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au
  • Ionic bonding: Transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal
  • Extraction of metals: Roasting (sulphide ores), Calcination (carbonate ores), Reduction
  • Corrosion: Iron rusting (4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O)
  • Galvanization: Coating iron with zinc layer to prevent rusting
  • Anodizing: Forming thick oxide layer on aluminium

Chapter 4 • Chemistry
Carbon and Its Compounds
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Covalent bond: Sharing of electron pairs between atoms
  • Carbon forms 4 covalent bonds (tetravalency)
  • Homologous series: Group of compounds with same functional group and similar chemical properties
  • Saturated hydrocarbons (Alkanes): CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (single bonds only)
  • Unsaturated hydrocarbons: Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ (double bond), Alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (triple bond)
  • Functional groups: -OH (Alcohol), -CHO (Aldehyde), -COOH (Carboxylic acid), -CO- (Ketone)
  • Ethanol (C₂H₅OH): Used as solvent, in medicines; harmful if consumed in excess
  • Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH): Vinegar is 5-8% acetic acid solution
  • Soaps: Sodium/potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids
  • Detergents: Sodium salts of sulphonic acids; work in hard water

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O (Combustion of methane)
C₂H₅OH + O₂ → CH₃COOH + H₂O (Oxidation of ethanol)
CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O
CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂
C₂H₅OH →(conc. H₂SO₄, 443K) C₂H₄ + H₂O (Dehydration)

Chapter 5 • Biology
Life Processes
7 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Nutrition: Autotrophic (photosynthesis in plants) and Heterotrophic (holozoic, saprophytic, parasitic)
  • Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O →(sunlight, chlorophyll) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Human digestive system: Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine
  • Respiration: Aerobic (with O₂, 38 ATP) and Anaerobic (without O₂, 2 ATP)
  • Human respiratory system: Nostrils → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Lungs → Alveoli
  • Transportation in humans: Heart (4 chambers), Blood (RBC, WBC, Platelets, Plasma)
  • Double circulation: Pulmonary (heart → lungs → heart) and Systemic (heart → body → heart)
  • Excretion: Kidneys filter blood → urine (nephron is basic unit)
  • Transportation in plants: Xylem (water, minerals - upward) and Phloem (food - translocation)

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
Aerobic respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (38 ATP)
Anaerobic respiration (yeast): C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + Energy (2 ATP)
Anaerobic respiration (muscles): C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ (Lactic acid) + Energy

Chapter 8 • Biology
Heredity
4 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Heredity: Transmission of characters from parents to offspring
  • Mendel's experiments with pea plants: 7 contrasting pairs of traits
  • Dominant trait: Expressed in F1 generation (e.g., Tall)
  • Recessive trait: Hidden in F1, appears in F2 (e.g., Dwarf)
  • Monohybrid cross: Tt × Tt → TT:Tt:tt = 1:2:1 (phenotype 3:1)
  • Dihybrid cross: 9:3:3:1 ratio in F2 generation
  • Sex determination: XX (female), XY (male); father determines sex of child
  • Genes: Units of inheritance located on chromosomes; DNA is the genetic material

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Monohybrid ratio: 3 Dominant : 1 Recessive (phenotypic)
Monohybrid ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (genotypic)
Dihybrid ratio: 9:3:3:1 (phenotypic)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

Chapter 9 • Physics
Light - Reflection and Refraction
7 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Laws of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection; incident ray, reflected ray, normal lie in same plane
  • Concave mirror: Converging mirror; used in torches, headlights, shaving mirrors
  • Convex mirror: Diverging mirror; used as rear-view mirror in vehicles
  • Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
  • Magnification: m = -v/u = h'/h
  • Sign convention: All distances measured from pole; along principal axis; incident light direction is positive
  • Refraction: Bending of light when passing from one medium to another
  • Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r; Refractive index n = c/v
  • Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
  • Power of lens: P = 1/f (in metres); unit is Dioptre (D)
  • Convex lens: Converging; used in magnifying glass, camera
  • Concave lens: Diverging; used in spectacles for myopia

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Magnification (mirror): m = -v/u = h'/h
Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
Magnification (lens): m = v/u = h'/h
Power of lens: P = 1/f (in metres), unit: Dioptre (D)
Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
Refractive index: n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in medium

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

Chapter 10 • Physics
The Human Eye and the Colourful World
3 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Human eye: Functions like a camera; cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina
  • Accommodation: Ability of eye to focus on objects at different distances
  • Near point: 25 cm (least distance of distinct vision)
  • Far point: Infinity for normal eye
  • Myopia (near-sightedness): Image forms before retina; corrected by concave lens
  • Hypermetropia (far-sightedness): Image forms behind retina; corrected by convex lens
  • Presbyopia: Loss of accommodation due to aging; corrected by bifocal lens
  • Dispersion of light: Splitting of white light into 7 colours (VIBGYOR) by prism
  • Scattering of light: Sky appears blue (Rayleigh scattering); sun appears red at sunrise/sunset
  • Tyndall effect: Scattering of light by colloidal particles

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Power of corrective lens for myopia: P = 1/f (f = far point distance, negative)
Power of corrective lens for hypermetropia: P = 1/f (calculated from near point)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. What is meant by accommodation of the eye?
  2. A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m distinctly. What should be the type and power of the corrective lens?
  3. Why does the sky appear blue?

Chapter 11 • Physics
Electricity
7 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Electric current: Flow of electric charge; I = Q/t; unit: Ampere (A)
  • Electric potential difference: V = W/Q; unit: Volt (V)
  • Ohm's law: V = IR (at constant temperature)
  • Resistance: R = V/I; unit: Ohm (Ω)
  • Factors affecting resistance: Length (directly proportional), Area (inversely), Material, Temperature
  • Resistivity: R = ρl/A; unit: Ω·m
  • Series combination: R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ (current same, voltage divides)
  • Parallel combination: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ (voltage same, current divides)
  • Electric power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R; unit: Watt (W)
  • Electrical energy: E = Pt = VIt; unit: kWh (1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J)
  • Heating effect of current: H = I²Rt (Joule's law)

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Current: I = Q/t
Ohm's law: V = IR
Resistance: R = ρl/A
Series: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
Parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R
Energy: E = Pt = VIt
Heating effect: H = I²Rt

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. An electric lamp of 100 Ω, a toaster of 50 Ω and a water filter of 500 Ω are connected in parallel. Calculate the equivalent resistance.

  4. How does the resistance of a wire vary with its area of cross-section?

Chapter 12 • Physics
Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
4 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Magnetic field: Region around a magnet where magnetic force is experienced
  • Magnetic field lines: Closed curves from N-pole to S-pole outside; never intersect
  • Oersted's experiment: Current-carrying conductor produces magnetic field
  • Right-hand thumb rule: Thumb shows current direction, curled fingers show magnetic field direction
  • Solenoid: Coil of wire that acts as a magnet when current flows; one end becomes N, other S
  • Electromagnet: Solenoid with soft iron core; strong temporary magnet
  • Force on current-carrying conductor in magnetic field: F = BIl (Fleming's Left-Hand Rule)
  • Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Forefinger (field), Middle finger (current), Thumb (force/motion)
  • Electric motor: Converts electrical energy to mechanical energy
  • Electromagnetic induction: Changing magnetic field induces EMF (Faraday's law)
  • Fleming's Right-Hand Rule: For direction of induced current in generator
  • AC vs DC: AC changes direction periodically (50 Hz in India); DC flows in one direction
  • Domestic electric circuits: Live (red), Neutral (black), Earth (green); 220V, 50Hz in India

🧮 Important Formulas & Equations

Force on conductor: F = BIl (B = magnetic field, I = current, l = length)
AC frequency in India: 50 Hz
Domestic supply: 220V AC

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  2. State Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.
  3. What is the principle of an electric motor?
  4. Name some devices in which electric motors are used.
  5. What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?

Chapter 1
Real Numbers
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Euclid's Division Lemma: a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b
  • Euclid's Division Algorithm: Finding HCF of two numbers
  • Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: Every composite number is a product of prime numbers
  • HCF × LCM = Product of two numbers
  • Irrational numbers: √2, √3, √5 are irrational (proof by contradiction)
  • Rational numbers have terminating or repeating decimal expansion
  • Terminating decimal: denominator has only 2 and 5 as prime factors

🧮 Important Formulas

Euclid's Lemma: a = bq + r (0 ≤ r < b)
HCF × LCM = a × b (for two numbers a, b)
HCF(a,b) = HCF using prime factorization

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Use Euclid's division algorithm to find the HCF of 135 and 225.
  2. Prove that √2 is irrational.
  3. Find the LCM and HCF of 6 and 20 by the prime factorization method.
  4. Check whether 6ⁿ can end with the digit 0 for any natural number n.
  5. Show that 3√2 is irrational.
Chapter 2
Polynomials
4 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Polynomial: Expression of the form aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ... + a₁x + a₀
  • Degree of polynomial: Highest power of variable
  • Zeroes of polynomial: Values of x where p(x) = 0
  • Linear polynomial: ax + b (1 zero)
  • Quadratic polynomial: ax² + bx + c (at most 2 zeroes)
  • Sum of zeroes (α + β) = -b/a
  • Product of zeroes (αβ) = c/a
  • Division algorithm: p(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x)

🧮 Important Formulas

Sum of zeroes: α + β = -b/a (quadratic ax² + bx + c)
Product of zeroes: αβ = c/a
For cubic: α + β + γ = -b/a, αβ + βγ + γα = c/a, αβγ = -d/a

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Find the zeroes of the polynomial x² - 3 and verify the relationship between zeroes and coefficients.
  2. Find a quadratic polynomial whose sum and product of zeroes are 1/4 and -1 respectively.
  3. Divide 3x² - x³ - 3x + 5 by x - 1 - x², and verify the division algorithm.
  4. If α and β are zeroes of x² + 7x + 12, find the value of 1/α + 1/β.
  5. Find all zeroes of 2x⁴ - 3x³ - 3x² + 6x - 2 if two of its zeroes are √2 and -√2.
Chapter 3
Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • General form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0
  • Graphical method: Lines intersect (unique solution), parallel (no solution), coincident (infinite solutions)
  • Consistent: Has at least one solution; Inconsistent: No solution
  • Substitution method: Express one variable in terms of other
  • Elimination method: Multiply equations to make coefficients equal, then subtract
  • Cross-multiplication method: x/(b₁c₂-b₂c₁) = y/(c₁a₂-c₂a₁) = 1/(a₁b₂-a₂b₁)
  • Condition for unique solution: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
  • Condition for no solution: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
  • Condition for infinite solutions: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

🧮 Important Formulas

Unique solution: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
No solution: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
Infinite solutions: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Solve: 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x - 4y = -24 by substitution method.
  2. For what values of k will the following pair have infinitely many solutions? kx + 3y - (k-3) = 0 and 12x + ky - k = 0
  3. Five years hence, the age of Jacob will be three times that of his son. Five years ago, Jacob's age was seven times that of his son. Find their present ages.
  4. The sum of a two-digit number and the number obtained by reversing the digits is 66. If the digits differ by 2, find the number.
  5. Solve graphically: x - y = 1 and 2x + y = 8.
Chapter 4
Quadratic Equations
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0)
  • Roots/solutions: Values of x that satisfy the equation
  • Factorization method: Splitting the middle term
  • Completing the square method
  • Quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b²-4ac)) / 2a
  • Discriminant: D = b² - 4ac
  • D > 0: Two distinct real roots
  • D = 0: Two equal real roots (x = -b/2a)
  • D < 0: No real roots

🧮 Important Formulas

Quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a
Discriminant: D = b² - 4ac
Sum of roots: -b/a
Product of roots: c/a

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

Chapter 5
Arithmetic Progressions
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • AP: Sequence where difference between consecutive terms is constant
  • Common difference: d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂
  • General term (nth term): aₙ = a + (n-1)d
  • Sum of first n terms: Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d] = n/2 [a + l]
  • If a, b, c are in AP then 2b = a + c
  • Sum of n natural numbers: n(n+1)/2

🧮 Important Formulas

nth term: aₙ = a + (n-1)d
Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2 (a + l), where l = last term
Sum of n natural numbers: n(n+1)/2

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Which term of the AP 3, 8, 13, 18,... is 78?
  2. Find the sum of first 22 terms of an AP in which d = 7 and 22nd term is 149.
  3. The first and the last terms of an AP are 17 and 350 respectively. If the common difference is 9, how many terms are there and what is their sum?
  4. Find the sum of first 24 terms of the AP whose nth term is given by aₙ = 3 + 2n.
  5. How many terms of AP 9, 17, 25,... must be taken to give a sum of 636?
Chapter 6
Triangles
8 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Similar triangles: Same shape but not necessarily same size
  • AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) similarity criterion
  • SAS (Side-Angle-Side) similarity criterion
  • SSS (Side-Side-Side) similarity criterion
  • Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT/Thales): If a line is parallel to one side of triangle and intersects other two sides, it divides them proportionally
  • Areas of similar triangles: Ratio of areas = square of ratio of corresponding sides
  • Pythagoras Theorem: In a right triangle, hypotenuse² = base² + perpendicular²
  • Converse of Pythagoras: If sum of squares of two sides equals square of third, triangle is right-angled

🧮 Important Formulas

BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC (if DE ∥ BC)
Area ratio: ar(△ABC)/ar(△DEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (AC/DF)²
Pythagoras: AC² = AB² + BC² (right angle at B)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
  2. Prove that the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  3. In the given figure, DE ∥ BC. Find EC if AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm and AE = 1 cm.
  4. Prove that in a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of squares of the other two sides.
  5. The sides of a triangle are 7 cm, 24 cm and 25 cm. Check if it is a right triangle.
Chapter 7
Coordinate Geometry
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Distance formula: Distance between (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂)
  • Section formula: Point dividing line segment in ratio m:n
  • Midpoint formula: Special case of section formula (m:n = 1:1)
  • Area of triangle using coordinates
  • Collinearity: Three points are collinear if area of triangle = 0

🧮 Important Formulas

Distance: d = √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)²]
Section formula: P = ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n))
Midpoint: M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Area of triangle: ½|x₁(y₂-y₃) + x₂(y₃-y₁) + x₃(y₁-y₂)|

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Find the distance between points A(2,3) and B(4,1).
  2. Find the coordinates of the point which divides the line segment joining A(1,-3) and B(-3,9) in the ratio 1:3 internally.
  3. Find the area of the triangle formed by the points (2,3), (1,0) and (7,0).
  4. In what ratio does the point (-4,6) divide the line segment joining A(-6,10) and B(3,-8)?

Chapter 8
Introduction to Trigonometry
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Trigonometric ratios: sin θ, cos θ, tan θ, cosec θ, sec θ, cot θ
  • sin θ = Perpendicular/Hypotenuse, cos θ = Base/Hypotenuse, tan θ = Perpendicular/Base
  • Trigonometric ratios of specific angles: 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°
  • sin 0° = 0, sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2, sin 90° = 1
  • Complementary angles: sin(90°-θ) = cos θ, tan(90°-θ) = cot θ
  • Trigonometric identities: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

🧮 Important Formulas

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
sin(90°-θ) = cos θ, cos(90°-θ) = sin θ
tan(90°-θ) = cot θ, cot(90°-θ) = tan θ

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. If tan A = 4/3, find the other trigonometric ratios of angle A.
  2. Prove that (sin θ - cos θ + 1)/(sin θ + cos θ - 1) = 1/(sec θ - tan θ).
  3. Evaluate: sin 60° cos 30° + sin 30° cos 60°.
  4. If tan 2A = cot(A - 18°), where 2A is an acute angle, find the value of A.
  5. Prove that (1 + tan²A)/(1 + cot²A) = tan²A.
Chapter 9
Some Applications of Trigonometry
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Angle of elevation: Angle between horizontal and line of sight (looking up)
  • Angle of depression: Angle between horizontal and line of sight (looking down)
  • Height and distance problems: Using trigonometric ratios to find heights/distances
  • tan θ is most commonly used in height-distance problems

🧮 Important Formulas

tan θ = Height / Distance (Perpendicular / Base)
Height = Distance × tan(angle of elevation)
Distance = Height / tan(angle of elevation)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. A tower stands vertically on the ground. From a point on the ground which is 15 m away from the foot, the angle of elevation of the top is 60°. Find the height.
  2. An observer 1.5 m tall is 28.5 m away from a chimney. The angle of elevation of the top from her eyes is 45°. What is the height of the chimney?
  3. From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Find the height of the tower.
  4. The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of the tower is 30° and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building.
  5. From a point P on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a 10 m tall building is 30°. Find the distance of point P from the foot of the building.

Circles

📖 Key Concepts

  • Tangent: Line touching circle at exactly one point
  • Point of tangency: Point where tangent touches the circle
  • Tangent is perpendicular to radius at point of contact
  • From an external point, two tangents can be drawn to a circle and they are equal in length
  • The angle between two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line segment joining the points of contact at the centre

Tangent ⊥ Radius (at point of contact)
PA = PB (tangents from external point P)
∠APB + ∠AOB = 180° (tangent-centre angle)

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

Surface Areas and Volumes
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Surface area and volume of combination of solids
  • Converting one type of solid into another (same volume)
  • Frustum of a cone: Formed by cutting a cone with a plane parallel to base

🧮 Important Formulas

Cylinder: CSA = 2πrh, TSA = 2πr(h+r), V = πr²h

Chapter 13
Statistics
6 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Mean of grouped data: Direct method, Assumed mean method, Step-deviation method
  • Median of grouped data: Middle value when data is arranged in order
  • Mode of grouped data: Value that appears most frequently
  • Cumulative frequency: Running total of frequencies
  • Ogive: Cumulative frequency graph (less than type, more than type)
  • Median from ogive: Point where N/2 meets the curve

🧮 Important Formulas

Mean (Direct): x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ / Σfᵢ
Mean (Assumed): x̄ = a + Σfᵢdᵢ / Σfᵢ (where dᵢ = xᵢ - a)
Median: l + [(N/2 - cf)/f] × h
Mode: l + [(f₁ - f₀)/(2f₁ - f₀ - f₂)] × h
Empirical relation: 3 Median = Mode + 2 Mean

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

Chapter 14
Probability
5 Marks +

📖 Key Concepts

  • Probability: Measure of likelihood of an event occurring
  • Experiment: Trial with well-defined outcomes
  • Sample space: Set of all possible outcomes
  • Event: Subset of sample space
  • P(E) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of outcomes
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
  • P(sure event) = 1, P(impossible event) = 0
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1 → P(not E) = 1 - P(E)
  • Playing cards: 52 cards (26 red, 26 black; 4 suits × 13 cards each)

🧮 Important Formulas

P(E) = Favourable outcomes / Total outcomes
P(not E) = 1 - P(E)
P(E) + P(Ē) = 1

❓ Important Questions (Board Exam Level)

  1. Two dice are thrown simultaneously. Find the probability of getting a sum of 7.
  2. A bag contains 3 red balls and 5 black balls. A ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that the ball drawn is (i) red (ii) not red.
  3. A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability of getting (i) a king of red colour (ii) a face card.
  4. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability of getting a queen of diamonds.
  5. If P(E) = 0.05, what is the probability of 'not E'?
